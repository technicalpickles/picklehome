# 802.11v BSS Transition Management: Research Report

## 1. What BSS Transition Management actually does

802.11v is a "Wireless Network Management" (WNM) amendment; BSS Transition Management (BTM) is one feature within it, alongside 802.11k (radio measurement/neighbor reports) and 802.11r (Fast BSS Transition, a separate amendment often bundled in vendor UIs under "Fast Roaming"). ([wifiwiki.wordpress.com](https://wifiwiki.wordpress.com/2020/04/27/802-11v-wireless-network-management/), [cwnp.com](https://www.cwnp.com/bandsteering/))

**The mechanism:**
- The AP sends a **BSS Transition Management Request** frame to an already-associated client. This can be *solicited* (client asked for candidates before roaming) or *unsolicited* (AP decides to nudge the client — e.g. because it's overloaded, or the client's RSSI/rate has dropped below a threshold). ([intowlan.com via search summary](https://www.intowlan.com/2018/08/80211v-wireless-network-management-bss-transition.html), [wifiwiki.wordpress.com](https://wifiwiki.wordpress.com/2020/04/27/802-11v-wireless-network-management/))
- The request carries a **candidate list** — zero or more Neighbor Report elements, each with a Preference value from 1–255 (255 = most preferred), giving the client a ranked set of BSSIDs/channels to consider. ([wifiwiki.wordpress.com](https://wifiwiki.wordpress.com/2020/04/27/802-11v-wireless-network-management/))
- A **Disassociation Imminent** bit can be set. If set, a Disassociation Timer field indicates how many beacon intervals remain before the AP will forcibly disassociate the client if it hasn't roamed. This is the closest thing to "forcing" a client — but even then, the client chooses *where* to go (or can reconnect to the same AP after being kicked, if it disagrees with the suggestion). ([wifiwiki.wordpress.com](https://wifiwiki.wordpress.com/2020/04/27/802-11v-wireless-network-management/))
- The client replies with a **BTM Response**: `Accept` (with the BSSID it's transitioning to) or `Reject` (with a reason code), or it can simply not respond/act at all. ([wifiwiki.wordpress.com](https://wifiwiki.wordpress.com/2020/04/27/802-11v-wireless-network-management/))

**Is it a hint or can the AP force it?** Multiple independent sources agree: **it's a suggestion, not enforcement**, *except* for the disassociation-imminent case which forces a disconnect (but still not a specific destination). "Clients receive BSS transition requests, including preferred APs, and decide whether to cooperate based on their own policies and capabilities." ([search summary via CWNP-adjacent sources](https://www.cwnp.com/bandsteering/)) The CWNP/band-steering explainer is explicit: "802.11v operates as a recommendation system, not enforcement... Client response varies — devices may accept recommendations or implement proprietary algorithms that ignore them." ([cwnp.com](https://www.cwnp.com/bandsteering/)) Apple's own docs confirm this from the client side: "roaming remains client-controlled... The network supplies information (via 802.11k, 802.11v) that informs device choices, but roaming remains client-controlled." ([support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628))

**Bottom line on mechanism:** BTM is advisory except that the AP can force a *disconnect* via disassociation-imminent; it cannot force a specific destination AP. A client is always free to reject the suggestion, or accept a kick and then simply re-associate to whatever AP it wants (including the one it was just told to leave), if the client's own scoring disagrees.

## 2. What factors AP vendors use to build BTM candidate lists

This is where real-world implementations diverge sharply from the "RSSI-only" mental model, and it's the crux of your situation.

- **UniFi/Ubiquiti:** Public documentation is thin on internals. Ubiquiti's own docs and third-party summaries describe BSS Transition as sharing "frequency information, BSSID addresses, and client connection counts per AP" with the client, i.e. it explicitly communicates per-AP client load, not just signal data. ([support.hostifi.com](https://support.hostifi.com/en/articles/10808664-what-is-bss-transition-in-unifi)) A blog on UniFi roaming describes the underlying handoff as based on "signal strength, network load, and other factors," collaboratively negotiated between device and network. ([deluisio.com](https://deluisio.com/networking/unifi/2024/08/19/optimizing-wi-fi-roaming-a-guide-to-bss-transition-in-unifi-networks/)) UniFi does **not** publish the weighting formula or expose per-decision reasoning in the standard controller UI — a real limitation noted in our own investigation (no `/stat/event` steering reason codes on CloudKey G2 legacy controllers).
- **Cisco Meraki** (best-documented vendor mechanism found, useful as a proxy for how "load-aware BTM" generally works): Meraki explicitly runs **pre-association balancing** (reject/redirect at association time via 802.11 status code 17) and, on MR 29+, **active/post-association balancing** using 802.11v BTM frames for 802.11v-capable clients. The algorithm considers **AP load** (client count) and a **proprietary link-quality metric** between the client and each candidate AP, but restricts candidates to a "resource group" — APs that can hear the client at ≥30 dB SNR — specifically so it won't steer a client to an AP that can't actually hear it well. Even so, Meraki's own docs don't disclose the exact load-vs-signal tradeoff weighting, and don't rule out steering to a technically-audible-but-weaker AP if it's sufficiently less loaded. ([documentation.meraki.com/MR/Other_Topics/Client_Balancing](https://documentation.meraki.com/MR/Other_Topics/Client_Balancing))
- **Aruba ClientMatch**: Continuously monitors client RSSI/SNR as reported, and load per radio, moving clients to prevent both "sticky" clients (RSSI too low) and radio overload — a genuinely hybrid RSSI+load steering engine, marketed as smarter/more conservative than basic 802.11v thresholds. ([arubanetworking.hpe.com/techdocs/aos/aos10/services/clientmatch](https://arubanetworking.hpe.com/techdocs/aos/aos10/services/clientmatch/))
- **Juniper Mist**: Uses a cloud AI/RRM engine that ingests client count, usage, interference, and adjusts power/channel/steering centrally rather than per-AP heuristics; two-tier (daily global optimization + ongoing local). This is the most "black box" of the compared vendors but explicitly load- and interference-aware, not purely RSSI. ([juniper.net RRM docs](https://www.juniper.net/documentation/us/en/software/mist/mist-wireless/topics/concept/mist-radio-resource-management.html))
- **General academic/patent literature** on 802.11v load balancing confirms the pattern industry-wide: implementations commonly build a weighted score from SNR/RSSI, channel utilization, client count, and supported bandwidth, with utilization/load often weighted to actively push clients away from busy APs — exactly the "load, not just RSSI" behavior suspected here. ([search summary of weighted load-balancing patent/whitepaper literature](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0908.4256))

**Takeaway for question 2:** No major vendor implementation, including UniFi's, is documented as pure-RSSI. All of them factor in AP/radio load, and several (Meraki, Aruba) explicitly describe scenarios where a less-loaded-but-farther AP can outscore a closer-but-busier one. This directly supports the hypothesis that UniFi's BTM steering could reasonably push a client from a 6-client, -67 dBm AP toward a 2-client, -83 dBm AP, if load is weighted heavily enough relative to signal in Ubiquiti's (undisclosed) algorithm.

## 3. How iOS handles BTM

Apple publishes real numbers here (via HT202628 / the current Apple platform deployment "Wi-Fi roaming" reference):

- **iPhone/iPad/Vision Pro roam trigger threshold: −70 dBm.** Below this, the device begins actively scanning for a better AP. Macs use −75 dBm. ([support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628))
- **Candidate strength requirement:** a candidate BSS must be **8 dB stronger** than the current AP while actively transmitting data, or **12 dB stronger** while idle, before iOS will actually roam to it. Macs require 12 dB stronger in both cases. ([support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628))
- iOS **uses 802.11k neighbor reports** to speed up scanning (checks the first six entries from the report rather than scanning every channel), and **uses 802.11v BTM information** — including AP-reported channel utilization and client counts — as an input into its own internal scoring of candidate APs, but the decision and final roam choice remain client-side. Apple's language: iOS "takes this information into account when deciding among the possible roam targets" — i.e., it's one input, not a directive. ([support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628))
- iOS also weighs Wi-Fi standard generation and channel width (prefers newer/wider) as tie-breakers, which can override pure signal proximity in some candidate comparisons — a documented quirk. ([support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT202628))
- **802.11r caveat:** there are widely reported compatibility issues between Apple devices and certain 802.11r ("Fast Transition") implementations — multiple UniFi community threads describe iPhones (especially newer models like the 13 Pro Max) failing to roam properly, getting stuck on far/weak APs, or needing SSID recreation/WPA3-only or PMF tuning to resolve, when Fast Roaming/802.11r is enabled. ([community.ui.com — "iPhone 13 Pro Max Roaming/Fast Transition doesn't work"](https://community.ui.com/questions/iPhone-13-Pro-Max-greater-Roaming-Fast-Transition-OTA-Adaptive-doesnt-work/f2518243-963b-41d4-9245-7e613006b4ba)) This is notable context for picklehome: **802.11r is already disabled**, which sidesteps that specific class of documented iOS pain and isolates BTM (802.11v) as the more likely single variable in the symptom seen here.

There's no publicly documented case of iOS "ignoring" BTM outright — it does honor/consider the request — but because iOS applies its own dB-margin gates (8/12 dB) on top of whatever the AP suggests, the AP's candidate ranking and iOS's own acceptance criteria can disagree, and either side (AP steering logic, or iOS's own threshold-based decision after being nudged) could plausibly be the actual cause of a bad roam.

## 4. Vendor and engineering guidance: enable vs. disable

- **Ubiquiti's own default posture is "leave it enabled."** Both the HostiFi (UI reseller/support partner) knowledge base and independent UniFi blogs describe BSS Transition as on by default and recommend keeping it on, framing it as generally beneficial and "very unlikely to be the cause" of roaming issues. ([support.hostifi.com](https://support.hostifi.com/en/articles/10808664-what-is-bss-transition-in-unifi)) None of the sources found present an official Ubiquiti document that says "disable BTM if X."
- **The more consistently-cited fix across UniFi guidance is Minimum RSSI, not BTM, and it's explicitly warned to be a blunt/risky tool.** Ubiquiti positions Min RSSI as a hard kick (disassociate below threshold, no advisory step) recommended mainly for **high-density** deployments, with warnings it can degrade real-time app (VoIP) experience if misconfigured, and community threads titled things like "Minimum RSSI kills roaming" reinforce that it's a two-edged sword, not a default-on recommendation. ([community summary of help.ui.com Minimum RSSI article + community.ui.com "Minimum RSSI kills roaming" thread](https://community.ui.com/questions/Minimum-RSSI-kills-roaming/14fb83eb-250a-4c1e-94dc-a38ea80b750e)) Recommended starting values across sources cluster around **−75 to −79 dBm** (never much lower). ([itman.ae](https://itman.ae/2025/07/02/how-unifi-handles-seamless-roaming/), [itproexpert.com](https://itproexpert.com/wi-fi-roaming-unifi-iphone-android/))
- **Ruckus's official troubleshooting guidance is the clearest documented statement that load-based steering itself is a known failure mode**, distinct from the BTM-specific question but directly on point: "Load balancing may force clients to roam to less congested APs, even if the current connection is stable. The recommendation is to disable this feature except in specific scenarios where devices are stationary and some APs are close to each other, such as offices." ([support.ruckuswireless.com/articles/000014303](https://support.ruckuswireless.com/articles/000014303)) This is vendor-agnostic confirmation that load-steering-causing-bad-roams is a recognized category of problem worth disabling for, not a fringe theory.
- **Tx power leveling is repeatedly cited as the "real" fix in the wireless engineering community, ahead of steering-feature toggles.** Multiple sources converge on: keep AP tx power roughly matched to client tx power (phones typically ≤15 dBm), avoid AP tx power exceeding client tx power by more than ~10 dB (asymmetric link — AP "shouts" but can't "hear" the phone's replies, which paradoxically causes clients to stay stuck since they still receive a strong beacon), and level power *across* APs in the same area so no single AP dominates coverage or is starved. ([metis.fi tx-power guidance](https://metis.fi/en/2017/10/txpower/), [search summary of Aruba RF/roaming VRD and community.cisco.com discussion](https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/HPE/MigratedAttachments/9B71B25A-80A1-4851-8B5F-21FDC9AB083F-2-VRD_Optimizing-WLAN-for-Roaming-Devices.pdf)) One UniFi-specific community thread on iPhone roaming explicitly recommends power leveling per band (e.g., 2G 10 dBm / 5G 17 dBm for tight cells, scaling up with more APs) as a primary lever, ahead of protocol toggles. ([community.ui.com "Poor Access Point Roaming - iPhones" — summarized via search](https://community.ui.com/questions/Poor-Access-Point-Roaming-iPhones/39a765b1-830d-47ef-b3bd-5f3dc442a24b))
- No CWNP-branded document with an explicit "disable BTM" recommendation was found; CWNP-adjacent material (cwnp.com's band-steering explainer) is descriptive/technical rather than prescriptive about enable/disable. This is a **gap** — no authoritative CWNP guidance either way was located; treat the "disable if it's misbehaving" position as coming from Ruckus's official docs and general community consensus, not CWNP specifically.

## 5. How to actually diagnose whether steering is helping or hurting

Given the confirmed blind spot on this network (no `/stat/event` reason codes on the legacy CloudKey G2 controller, no per-AP RSSI visibility for a client not currently associated), here's what the sourced methodology suggests:

- **Over-the-air packet capture (Wireshark) is the standard ground-truth method**, independent of controller API limitations. Revolution Wi-Fi's methodology: capture simultaneously on the channels of the APs involved (ideally with 2–3 adapters/monitor-mode devices), filter for association/reassociation/action frames (`wlan.fc.type_subtype == 0x00 || 0x02`, plus BTM Action frames), and measure the full roam timeline (probe time → auth → assoc → key derivation). Crucially, this captures the actual BTM Request frame **content** — the candidate list and preference values the AP is sending — which is exactly the visibility the legacy controller lacks. This would tell you definitively whether the AP is issuing a BTM request nudging the phone toward the 2nd-floor AP, and what candidate list/preferences it's offering. ([revolutionwifi.blogspot.com](http://revolutionwifi.blogspot.com/2013/01/wi-fi-roaming-analysis-with-wireshark.html))
- Revolution Wi-Fi's own case study is a good diagnostic template: they found an AP was suppressing probe responses at low SNR, which *worsened* roam behavior (376ms probing) — they identified this purely from OTA capture and fixed it by disabling the offending feature. Same pattern applies to identifying bad BTM candidate lists. ([revolutionwifi.blogspot.com](http://revolutionwifi.blogspot.com/2013/01/wi-fi-roaming-analysis-with-wireshark.html))
- **Practical proxy metrics without full packet capture:** watch UniFi's per-client "Uplink" history/graph across a roam event for the roam-then-slow-climb-back pattern already observed live, and correlate roam timestamps against each AP's client-count graph. If roams to the 2nd-floor AP cluster right when its client count drops to 0–1 (making it "attractive" on load) while its RSSI to the phone is still poor, that's strong circumstantial evidence for load-weighted steering, even without seeing the actual BTM frame.
- **A/B test by toggling BSS Transition off** (config change): if the bad roams stop when BTM is disabled and resume when re-enabled, that's a clean causal test — more reliable than packet analysis alone given the ambiguity in what UniFi's algorithm actually weighs.
- **Ruckus's diagnostic framing is also useful as a checklist**: scope the problem (all areas/devices, or specific?), then look for the successful-roam frame sequence (Auth req → Auth resp → Reassoc req → Reassoc resp success) versus failure/rejection patterns. ([support.ruckuswireless.com/articles/000014303](https://support.ruckuswireless.com/articles/000014303))

## 6. Documented cases of exactly this symptom (bounce to farther/less-loaded AP)

- **Ruckus's official troubleshooting doc is the closest verbatim documented case**, describing load balancing forcing roams to "less congested APs, even if the current connection is stable," as a known, named failure mode with an explicit disable-except-narrow-cases recommendation. ([support.ruckuswireless.com/articles/000014303](https://support.ruckuswireless.com/articles/000014303))
- **Meraki's docs implicitly document the same risk** by explaining that their algorithm restricts candidates to APs that can hear the client at ≥30 dB SNR specifically to avoid steering into unreachable/poor APs — an engineering safeguard that only makes sense if the underlying load-steering logic would otherwise happily pick a weak-but-idle AP. That safeguard is exactly the guardrail that, if UniFi's BTM implementation lacks an equivalent, would explain the symptom seen here (2nd-floor AP under load-based scoring, without an RSSI floor gate). ([documentation.meraki.com](https://documentation.meraki.com/MR/Other_Topics/Client_Balancing))
- **UniFi community forum threads document the adjacent-but-distinct "sticky client" and "iPhone won't roam / rejects Fast Transition" failure modes extensively**, but no UniFi community thread with an unambiguous, fully-readable narrative matching this exact scenario (bounce onto a farther, more-idle AP, confirmed as caused by BTM specifically) was found. The Ubiquiti community pages (`community.ui.com`) are JavaScript-rendered and returned only "Loading Ubiquiti Community" to the fetch tool in every attempt — direct read access to those threads' full text was not available in this research pass, only search-engine snippets. Threads whose titles strongly suggest this exact symptom exist (e.g. "BSS transition on IOT Wifi network - good and bad," "WiFi clients not roaming to better signal access points on their own (WiFi 6 / BSS Transition / Fast Roaming)," "Poor Access Point Roaming - iPhones") but their content could not be verified/quoted here — **flagging this as a real source-access gap**, not a claim of absence. Opening those URLs directly in an authenticated/JS-capable browser would fill this in.

## Bottom line for this specific situation

The research **supports the hypothesis** in the general sense: no vendor's BTM/steering implementation (Meraki, Aruba, and by inference UniFi) is RSSI-only — all factor in AP load, and Ruckus explicitly documents this exact class of bug (load balancing steering to a farther, less-loaded AP even when the current link is fine) as a known, named failure mode worth disabling for. Given picklehome's 6/6/2-client split and matching tx-power imbalance (13/14/16 dBm — the 2nd-floor AP is both least loaded *and* transmitting loudest, a double pull toward it), a load-aware BTM implementation choosing that AP as "preferred" is entirely plausible even at −83 dBm actual signal.

That said, the research points to a **combination fix, not just "disable BTM"**:

1. **Tx power leveling is the more strongly and consistently recommended first move** across sources (Metis.fi, Aruba RF/roaming VRD, UniFi community threads) — bring the 2nd-floor AP's 16 dBm down closer to the two 13–14 dBm APs so it isn't artificially "louder" than its actual client-serving value, and so its beacon doesn't out-shout its real coverage quality. This addresses the root asymmetry without giving up any of BTM's legitimate benefits (faster handoffs via 802.11k-informed scanning, which iOS does use).
2. **BSS Transition itself is generally recommended to stay on** by Ubiquiti-adjacent sources, and disabling it removes real benefit (faster roam via candidate pre-scanning) for a fix that's really targeting the load-weighting behavior, not BTM's core mechanism. Consider it a second-line experiment (the clean A/B test described in §5), not the first lever to pull.
3. **Min RSSI is explicitly warned against as a first tool** — it's a blunt hard-kick UniFi itself scopes to high-density deployments, and misconfiguring it (too aggressive) is a separately documented cause of *excessive* roaming. Not recommended as the primary fix given only 5 APs and a fairly sparse home deployment.
4. Given 802.11r is already off here, the most commonly-cited iOS/UniFi compatibility failure mode is already avoided — this makes it more likely that what remains (BTM candidate-list weighting vs. tx power imbalance) is the real driver, which the tx-power-leveling recommendation targets directly.

**Recommended fix ordering per the research: (1) level tx power down on the 2nd-floor AP (Upstairs AC HD) first, re-test; (2) if the bounce persists, disable BSS Transition as an A/B test with OTA/Wireshark capture to confirm causality before committing to it long-term; (3) treat Min RSSI as a last resort, not a first move.**
